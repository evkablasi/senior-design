As such, data protected by HIPAA and ERISA regulations exist in the same source control system which we will be using to work on our project. ## Summary
To ensure that our project does not mishandle any data, we first must define HIPAA and ERISA and the security laws that exist around them. The Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996 (HIPAA) was created as a response to the growing use of technology for medical transactions and storing health information, and thus the growing need for privacy regulations [^1]. An amendment added in 2003, The Security Rule, goes into more detail about the responsibilities of companies to protect this information, which includes ensuring confidentiality and integrity, as well as anticipating any possible threats or breaches [^2]. The Employee Retirement Income Security Act of 1974 (ERISA) sets standards and guidelines surrounding private-sector employee benefits, like retirement and health plans[^3]. The money and information involved in ERISA protected data could be so valuable that the United States Department of Labor created a document of best cybersecurity practices to keep this data safe, which emphasizes access controls, risk assessment, and data encryption [^4]. 

## As it Applies to Senior Design
In terms of how to apply HIPAA regulations and safety to our database, it is best to turn to the breakdown of administrative, physical, and technical safeguards. Physical safeguards include workstation use and security and device and media controls. I believe this is already covered by the laptops SecurKart has provided us with, seeing as all the work on creating our product is sequestered to these approved and secure devices that already live within the SecurKart system. This aspect is not entirely under our control, as we are depending on pre-existing security, but it is in our hands to use these devices appropriately and refrain from transferring any information, whether it be database design or source code, to our personal devices. Most of the technical safeguards, namely access control, authentication, and transmission security, deal with ensuring that only authorized users are able to access protected health information in all cases of where the data may exist. We accomplished these by setting up multi-factor authentication for our accounts. This means that not only if a person were able to electronically access this data, but even if they were able to bypass physical safeguards i.e. get their hands on one of our SecurKart laptops, they would hit a wall almost immediately. Another technical safeguard demands the protection of protected health information from being altered or destroyed. To comply with this, we will use a test network for all testing while developing our database, so that no other aspects of the source control system will be impacted. Administrative safeguards overlap a bit with technical and physical regarding access management and security management, but the one that stands out to me is security awareness and training, which calls for education for all employees on security policies and procedures. It occurs to me that we may need more information from SecurKart on how protected health information exists in their network and how our work could possibly impact it, which, depending on the extent, could be pertinent to ensuring that we respect HIPAA regulations during our developing.
